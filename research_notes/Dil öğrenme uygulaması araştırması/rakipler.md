# Competing language-learning apps and independent evidence (checked 2026-11-02)

Evidence labels used below:
- **[COMPANY]**: funded or commissioned by the vendor, or vendor staff are authors.
- **[INDEPENDENT]**: no vendor funding found. Funding was often not checked in full, so see the caveats.
- **[STORE]**: read directly from the Turkish App Store page on 2026-11-02.
- **[AGGREGATOR]**: third-party site, low confidence.

Read-depth caveat: many study details come from abstracts, search summaries or secondary reports, not the full paper. This is noted where it matters.

## 1. What each app does, its AI features and its Turkish support

### Takeaway
Turkish appearing in a store's language list does not prove usable Turkish support. Speak lists Turkish, yet its developer told reviewers Turkish was not supported. AI speaking practice is now the main area of competition, and it sits behind a paywall. A real Turkish-medium free course is less common than the store lists suggest. English Ninjas is the only Turkish-founded player I could verify, and it was sold to a foreign company in 2021. **Wordela is not shown to be Turkish.**

### Inferences
- A store language tag should be treated as a claim to test, not a fact. A competitor audit needs hands-on testing of the English-from-Turkish path in each app.
- The Praktika approach of a tutor that can use the learner's native language looks like the most relevant paid pattern for Turkish beginners. This is my reading, not a tested result.

### Gaps
- No hands-on app testing was done. Turkish explanation quality, course depth and CEFR ceiling are unverified for every app.
- Method descriptions for Anki and LingQ rest on thin sourcing here. No AI feature review was done for Busuu, Memrise, Babbel, Cake, LingQ or Cambly as of 2026.
- I found no reliable market share data for language apps in Turkey and no complete list of Turkish-made apps. "No others exist" cannot be concluded.

## 3. Efficacy evidence: company-funded versus independent

### Takeaway
The strongest headline claims are company-linked and rest on motivated, educated, self-selected volunteers. Independent studies are small, and they mostly show that learning gains track time spent. Long-run usage is the main practical limit. No independent study was found for Speak or Praktika, and none found measures English learned from Turkish.

### Cited findings

**Company-funded or company-authored**
- **Vesselinov & Grego 2012, Duolingo [COMPANY]:** commissioned by Duolingo, with data analysis described as independent; 34 hours per first-semester equivalent claimed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/01/17/study-learning-spanish-with-duolingo-can-be-more-effective-than-college-classes-or-rosetta-stone/amp)). Limits:
  - Internet-ad volunteers, mean age 35, 69% college graduates; SD of 12.1 around a mean gain of 8.1 points per hour ([Avant-hosted paper](https://rebrand.avantassessment.com/wp-content/uploads/The-effectiveness-of-Duolingo-in-developing-receptive-and-productive-language-knowledge-and-proficiency.pdf)).
  - Krashen says only 90 of 156 volunteers finished 30 hours and 25% did 8 hours or less ([Krashen letter](https://mail.sdkrashen.com/content/articles/research_on_duolingo.pdf)). These numbers come from a critic and were not checked against the report.
- **Jiang et al. 2021, Duolingo [COMPANY]:** 225 US-resident beginners using Duolingo only, published in *Foreign Language Annals*; concludes the app can be effective ([NAU record](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/evaluating-the-reading-and-listening-outcomes-of-beginning-level-/)). Duolingo's release claims learners who finished five units matched four university semesters on reading and listening ([release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2022/01/12/2365800/0/en/Leading-Language-Research-Journal-Publishes-Study-Showing-Duolingo-Learning-Outcomes-Are-Comparable-to-University-Classes.html)). The first author was Duolingo's lead learning scientist, and critics note 90% of the 2020 sample held at least an undergraduate degree ([Slavic](https://www.benslavic.com/?p=25401)). This covers only reading and listening, not speaking, and only learners who finished.
- **Babbel: Loewen, Isbell & Sporn 2020 [COMPANY]:** funded by Babbel's parent, Lesson Nine ([MedicalResearch](https://medicalresearch.com/can-you-really-learn-a-language-using-babbel/)). 85 enrolled, 54 completed ([Babbel](https://uk.babbel.com/press/en-gb/releases/2019-01-10-Babbel-SPA-2018-Study.html)), which is 36% attrition among students. No control group. Reported oral-improvement rates conflict: 59% ([hrnews](https://www.hrnews.cz/human-resources/learning-and-development-id-2698897/study-how-effective-are-foreign-language-learning-applicatio-id-3938468?setlang=en)) versus almost 69% ([interview](https://medicalresearch.com/can-you-really-learn-a-language-using-babbel/)). Study time was the strongest predictor.
- **Busuu [COMPANY]:** commissioned from CUNY's Queens College; "22 hours equals one college semester" ([Busuu](https://www.busuu.com/en/research/university-study)). Same lead researcher as the Duolingo study ([Busuu blog](https://blog.busuu.com/?p=19949), search summary). Sample figures vary within Busuu's own materials: 196 versus 144 ([blog](https://blog.busuu.com/busuu-university-study/)).
- **Praktika:** site claims "private-tutor results" with no study cited ([site](https://praktika.ai/)). **Speak:** no study found (search of [review sites](https://www.issen.com/blog/speak-app-alternatives/) and press; absence is not proof).

**Independent, or not known to be company-funded**
- **Loewen et al. 2019, *ReCALL* [funding not verified]:** nine participants learning Turkish on Duolingo for a semester. Improvement, moderate correlation with time spent, varied motivation, frustration with materials ([oa.mg record](https://oa.mg/work/10.1017/s0958344019000065)).
- **Nielson 2011, *LL&T* [INDEPENDENT, funding not read]:** US government employees using Rosetta Stone and TELL ME MORE. "Severe participant attrition" despite high initial motivation, tied to technical issues and weak support for self-study ([abstract](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/handle/10125/44265)). A secondary source reports only 14% used the software for over 10 hours despite a 200-hour commitment, with one learner finishing ([Ask Dr. Katie](https://getengen.com/blog-posts/ask-dr-katie-can-an-app-really-teach-me-a-language)). **Full paper was blocked, so these figures are secondary.** Old and not about smartphone apps, but the most useful attrition result found.
- **Seibert Hanson & Brown 2019/2020, *CALL* [INDEPENDENT]:** Anki with 62 beginning Spanish students in a semester course. Title calls it "an effective but bitter pill". Many students did not study regularly even though it was easy to access and a required assignment; complaints included reviewing already-tested words ([paper](https://andymatuschak.org/files/papers/Seibert%20Hanson%20and%20Brown%20-%202020%20-%20Enhancing%20L2%20learning%20through%20a%20mobile%20assisted%20sp.pdf)).
- **Zhou & Zhou 2026, *ReCALL* 38(1) [INDEPENDENT]:** 65 (quasi-)experimental vocabulary studies; d = 1.28 at 10+ weeks. Publication bias was detected and the authors warn the effect may be inflated ([paper](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/79C8F00E48D521ED3BCFE0625B5549F3/S0958344025100335a.pdf/metaanalysis_on_mobileassisted_vocabulary_learning_do_mobile_applications_help.pdf)).
- **Mihaylova et al. 2022 [INDEPENDENT]:** 23 studies, moderate to strong overall effect, but high risk of bias and low-quality evidence ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9479751/)).
- **Olsen et al.:** 21 of the reviewed articles (81%) reported effectiveness; described in search results as a manuscript, and the page was blocked ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358538758_A_systematic_review_examining_the_efficacy_of_commercially_available_foreign_language_learning_mobile_apps)). Publication bias likely applies, in my view.
- **Mobile games meta-analysis 2025:** 38 studies, 4,102 participants, g = 0.962 ([Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09588221.2025.2528786)). Short-term gain from games is not the same as long-term proficiency, in my view.
- **ELSA:** mostly small pre/post or quasi-experimental studies from Indonesia and Iran, largely regional journals ([IDEAS](https://ejournal.iainpalopo.ac.id/index.php/ideas/article/view/6955), [Indonesian EFL Journal](https://doaj.org/article/ee689d243ddb47269c82b6c4d909d501)). The PLOS ONE paper measures satisfaction, not gains ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11717304/)). Funding not checked.
- **Duolingo perceptions:** liked for gamified engagement; criticized for excess gamification, thin explicit grammar and unrealistic content ([Ghasemi & Kouhdasht 2024](https://tale.razi.ac.ir/article_3080_9a23dc213793624361ea2d0945115853.pdf)).

### Inferences
- Reading and vocabulary evidence is stronger than speaking evidence. The "one semester" framing compares volunteers who finished with a different population, so it should not be copied into marketing.
- Meta-analytic effect sizes likely overstate real-world results, because studies use assigned, supervised use. Spaced repetition looks worthwhile, but compliance is the real constraint: even a required assignment did not secure use.
- Any evaluation of a new app should report starters as well as finishers, because finisher-only results hide the main failure.

### Gaps
- **Duolingo English Test research was not reviewed.** It would show the validity of a test, not that the course teaches English (my inference).
- Not read, worth opening: Shortt et al. 2023 *CALL* review of Duolingo literature (citation seen only, [list](https://eprints.umpo.ac.id/21371/8/DAFTAR%20PUSTAKA.pdf)); the [*CALICO* study of Duolingo English courses](https://journal.equinoxpub.com/Calico/article/view/26704), likely the closest fit for English learners; [McQuillan 2019](https://mail.sdkrashen.com/content/articles/and_then_there_were_none_mcquillan_2019.pdf) on attrition.
- Funding disclosures of Loewen 2019 and Nielson 2011 were not confirmed from full text. Whether Loewen's nine are survivors of a bigger group is unverified.
- No randomized long-term trial with an active control for a commercial app was found. No efficacy study was found for Memrise, Cake, Cambly, LingQ, Speak or Praktika, though search was limited.
- No study of Turkish speakers learning English through any of these apps was found.

## 4. Retention, completion and criticism

### Takeaway
Duolingo shows very strong habit metrics (58.7M DAU, 84% CURR), but no public metric shows proficiency reached or course completion. Engagement is not learning. Its energy system shows the tension of a free tier serving a subscription business. Other vendors publish almost nothing comparable.

### Cited findings
- **Duolingo Q2 2026 [COMPANY, filed with the SEC]:** DAU 58.7M (+23% YoY), MAU 140.6M (+10%), paid subscribers 12.7M (+17%), CURR a record 84% (about +1 pt YoY), revenue $298.5M ([letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm)). Part of the DAU acceleration was attributed to a one-time Streak Revival event (same source).
- **Q1 2026:** DAU 56.5M, MAU 137.8M, paid 12.5M ([letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026029790/q1fy26duolingo3-31x26share.htm)).
- **Q3 2026 letter not found as of 2026-11-02** ([search of filings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053603/duol-20260630.htm)). It may appear soon, so re-check.
- **Strategy:** in 2026 Duolingo prioritized user growth and learning outcomes and seeks revenue "without adding friction to free users"; Q3 revenue guidance of about $302M, +11.1% ([letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm)). Reuters-sourced coverage says this was just below the roughly $304M expected ([Boursorama](https://www.boursorama.com/bourse/actualites-amp/les-previsions-de-chiffre-d-affaires-de-duolingo-inferieures-aux-attentes-font-oublier-les-resultats-trimestriels-superieurs-aux-previsions-3445ba9994ee43bdd6d04f804bd48e73)).
- **Energy system (reported May 2025):**
  - Each exercise costs one unit, mistakes cost energy too, the bar holds 25, progress earns random refills, and subscribers get unlimited energy. A Duolingo engineer says users stay active longer, since they no longer exit after losing all hearts ([Teknoblog](https://www.teknoblog.com/duolingo-enerji-sistemi-kalp-yerine/)).
  - Critics say it pushes free users toward subscribing; a [change.org petition](https://www.change.org/p/duolingo-hearts-not-energy) objects. This is low-authority evidence of complaint, not of scale.
- **AI-first backlash (2025):** CEO email said about 100 courses took around 12 years, versus about 150 in one year with AI; widely read as replacing workers; Duolingo said AI is a tool, not a replacement for learning experts ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/whats-the-duolingo-ai-first-controversy-the-ceos-pro-ai-message-and-the-online-backlash-explained)). No independent measure of users leaving was found.
- **Memrise:** removing community courses from the main app drew user complaints ([Kimola](https://kimola.com/reports/uncover-the-impact-memrise-apps-user-feedback-report-app-store-us-147885)).
- **Billing complaints:**
  - Praktika reviewer reports an unexpected $110 charge ([search result, Trustpilot](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/praktika.ai?page=6); exact page not pinned). Anecdote only.
  - Cambly: complaints about price rises in Turkey ([Şikayetvar](https://www.sikayetvar.com/cambly/cambly-uyelik-fiyatlarindaki-asiri-artis)) and no refunds on 12-month discounted plans, per one guide ([Gazete Birlik](https://www.gazetebirlik.com/fiyatlar/cambly-fiyatlari/905537), not pinned).
  - ELSA: a Capterra reviewer complains about login, value and bugs ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/240698/ELSA-Speak/)). Single review.
- **Speak "lags Duolingo" on offering,** per eMarketer's 2024 note ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/speak-reaches-unicorn-status-ai-language-tutoring--offering-lags-behind-duolingo)). Dated.

### Inferences
- My calculations from the Q2 figures: DAU/MAU about 41.7% (58.7/140.6), up from about 37.2% a year earlier (47.7/128.3); paid/MAU about 9.0%. The large majority of Duolingo's monthly users are not paying.
- Streaks work as retention devices, and a one-off revival event was large enough for management to name it. Copying retention mechanics is cheap, but it can also measure the habit rather than learning.
- Removing user-created decks caused backlash at Memrise, so a small app should avoid taking away content people built.

### Gaps
- No public, audited course-completion rate was found for any app.
- CURR's exact definition was not re-read here; it is a company metric covering current users, not new users.
- No day-30 retention, churn or subscriber data was found for Babbel, Busuu, Speak, Praktika, ELSA, Memrise, Cambly, Cake or LingQ.
- No systematic analysis of Turkish-language store reviews was done, so user complaint patterns here are anecdotal.

## 5. Gaps these apps leave for Turkish speakers: copy, avoid, do differently

### Takeaway
The clearest documented need is spoken and written output: in EF's 2025 data, Türkiye's speaking and writing scores trail reading by more than 60 points. Turkish-specific pronunciation problems and speaking anxiety are well described. None of the evidence found shows a competitor targeting them with Turkish-medium feedback, though that was not hands-on tested.

### Cited findings
- **EF EPI 2025, Türkiye:** rank #71, score 488 (down 9), which equals the global average of 488. Reading 503, listening 478, writing 441, speaking 438. Regional spread: Marmara 500, Eastern Anatolia 446 ([EF fact sheet](https://www.ef.com/assetscdn/WIBIwq6RdJvcD9bc8RMd/cefcom-epi-site/fact-sheets/2025/ef-epi-fact-sheet-turkey-english.pdf)). **Limit:** a vendor-run index from 2.2M EF SET takers ([EF](https://www.ef.edu/about-us/press/articles/2025/ef-english-proficiency-index-2025-launched/)), so not a representative sample.
- **Pronunciation:**
  - Turkish learners' persistent problems include /θ/–/t/, /ð/–/d/, /w/–/v/ and vowels; word stress and vowel reduction errors are linked to transfer and spelling, as Turkish spelling is regular ([Levis & Uzun 2026](https://www.iastatedigitalpress.com/psllt/article/id/21224/), [Uzun 2022](https://mextesol.net/journal/public/files/23128470e8cec8fe5ce66bdb7f4ac99e.pdf), [IOJET](https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/iojet/iojet-457.pdf)).
  - These are small, local studies seen via search summaries. The exact pairing of each claim to each link was not fully checked.
- **Anxiety:**
  - 596 learners at a Turkish state university: pronunciation anxiety was higher among women, less proficient learners, those never abroad and those with less study time ([Tekten 2020, Bilkent thesis](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/items/68de443a-8240-4d86-ac67-4c595aa5818c/full)).
  - Most of 232 tertiary learners felt speaking anxiety to some degree ([Genç et al. 2016](https://avesis.inonu.edu.tr/yayin/372f1364-f56d-46a3-8b57-51b8fa1fcdf9/foreign-language-speaking-anxiety-and-anxiety-coping-strategies-employed-by-turkish-efl-learners)).
  - A study of 383 preparatory students names pronunciation, immediate questions and fear of mistakes and negative evaluation as main causes ([probable source](https://abakus.inonu.edu.tr/items/c17b8410-6662-42cc-a51c-88532c7895ce); link pairing not confirmed).
- **Praktika's native-language tutoring** and tone setting (Soft, Balanced, Strict) directly address beginners and anxiety ([site](https://praktika.ai/)), but Turkish is not mentioned.
- **Speak** told users Turkish was not yet supported ([TR store](https://apps.apple.com/tr/app/speak-language-learning/id1286609883)), and **ELSA** lists English only ([TR store](https://apps.apple.com/tr/app/elsa-speak-english-learning/id1083804886)). The two speaking specialists are therefore the weakest on documented Turkish support.
- **Live tutor pricing** puts Cambly lessons at about ₺450 or more each, per one report ([Haber Gazetesi](https://www.habergazetesi.com.tr/gercek-cambly-ucret-ne-kadar-reklamlardaki-300-tllik-dersler-ne-kadar-gercekci)).

### Inferences (recommendations, not findings)
**Copy**
- Short daily sessions and a forgiving streak. Duolingo's results suggest habit mechanics work, and study time predicts gains across the Duolingo and Babbel studies.
- Spaced repetition, with short review sessions. Evidence favors it, but it must be tied to real tasks, since students dislike bare review.
- Tutor tone control and native-language help for beginners, as Praktika offers.

**Avoid**
- Limits on practice tied to mistakes or exercise counts. A free app has no subscriber reason for energy, and the mechanism discourages practice.
- "Same as X semesters" claims, or claims based on finishers only.
- Multi-tier confusing pricing, and annual lock-ins that drive complaints.
- Removing user-created material.
- Reward loops that can be cleared without producing language.

**Do differently**
- Explanations in Turkish written for Turkish problems: word order, articles, sounds missing in Turkish, and stress and vowel reduction (from the transfer findings).
- Lead with output: short private speaking and writing exercises for Turkish A1 to B1 learners, in a low-pressure setting without public scores. This matches the weakest EF scores and the anxiety findings.
- Minimal pair and stress drills for /θ/, /ð/, /w/–/v/, /æ/–/ʌ/ with Turkish-language feedback.
- Measure honestly: publish the share of starters still active and pre/post results, including dropouts. Competitors publish almost nothing like this, so even a small dataset would stand out.
- Design for free costs. Live AI speech is what rivals charge for, so a free app needs a cost cap: on-device or limited-use speech practice and scripted exercises first. Cost modelling was outside this research.

### Gaps
- No competitor was tested for Turkish-specific pronunciation feedback, so "no one does this" is **not established**.
- No Turkish user survey, demographic breakdown or willingness-to-pay data was found.
- Whether free AI tools such as general chatbots already fill the speaking gap was not researched, and they may be the true competitor for a free app.
- The accuracy of speech recognition for Turkish-accented English in these apps was not found.
