# Turkish L1 speakers learning English: errors, pronunciation, vocabulary, anxiety, exams, market

Research date: 2 November 2026. Evidence labels used below:
- **[full text]** I read the paper or document.
- **[abstract]** I read only the abstract or record.
- **[snippet]** I saw only a search-result summary, so verify before quoting.
- **WEAK** means a small sample, a single class, an unpublished or conference paper, or a commercial source.

## Which errors do corpus and error-analysis studies document, how frequent are they, and what is missing from the app's tag list?

### Takeaway
No published TICLE error-frequency table turned up, so the best numbers come from smaller Turkish learner corpora. These agree on the top group (verb form and agreement, prepositions, articles, noun number, word choice), but their rankings differ with taxonomy and level. The biggest gaps in the app's tags are word choice and collocation, wrong-verb-form errors, the extra-article direction, for/to, subordinate-clause fragments, and "I've been lived"-type auxiliary mixing.

### Cited Findings

**Largest and most usable corpus**
- **Gazioğlu & Aydın 2024 (LECOSE) [full text]:** 506 essays, about 190,000 tokens, 208 prep-school learners at B1-B2 at one state university, ages 17-35. Interrater agreement was checked on only a small sample — [JLR 8(2)](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/4229454)
- Main error types in the same study:

| Type | Level 2 (n=4,215) | Level 3 (n=3,916) |
|---|---|---|
| Misselection | 48.20% | 43.36% |
| Omission | 28.94% | 30.97% |
| Overinclusion | 14.25% | 16.36% |
| Misordering | 4.43% | 4.74% |
| Blends | 4.15% | 4.54% |

  Source: [Gazioğlu & Aydın 2024](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/4229454)
- By grammar element, the order was verb conjugation, prepositions, articles, grammatical number, then voice. More than half of article errors were omissions. Level 2 tended to omit articles and Level 3 tended to insert unnecessary ones — [same](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/4229454)
- Students reused in/on/at interchangeably, which the authors tie to Turkish -de/-da. They also confused for/to, which the authors tie to Turkish "için". Many left out needed prepositions — [same](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/4229454)
- Corrective feedback over one term produced no significant drop at Level 2 (t=1.22, p≈.22) and only slight improvement at Level 3 — [same](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/4229454)

**Academic writing, English majors**
- **Demirel 2017 [full text]:** 150 essays, 99,352 words, 45 first-year English Language and Literature students at one university, convenience sample — [ELT 10(10)](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1157960.pdf)
- Share of all errors: extra article 8.3%; missing article 7.8%; spelling 7.8%; noun number in NP 7.8%; subject-verb agreement 7.1%; verbal sub-structure 4.2%; word form 4.2%; fragments 4.2%; missing preposition 3.9%; wrong preposition 3%; tense choice 3%; lexical verb choice 3%. Verb errors overall were 26.3% — [same](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1157960.pdf)
- Agreement errors run in both directions: "those who downloads", "this student do not", "every historian have" — [same](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1157960.pdf)
- Within clause errors, fragments were 54.8%, often a Because-clause standing alone, and run-ons 9.7%. Within adjective errors, lexical choice was 52.6% — [same](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1157960.pdf)

**Word choice as the dominant error (WEAK, n=17)**
- **Erkaya 2012 [full text]:** 17 essays by 16 male and 1 female freshmen. Word choice 45.0% (190), articles 15.4% (65), prepositions 10.0% (42), punctuation 8.2% (35), noun plural 7.1% (30), spelling 7.0% (29), tense 2.8% (12), fragments 2.6% (11), subject-verb agreement 1.9% (8) — [MEXTESOL 36(2)](https://www.mextesol.net/journal/public/files/2996274430055836fdb4163281d420ec.pdf)
- Examples: "go a cold city", "there are lots of department", "there are seven region", "I've been lived", "Eskişehir is known that a very cheap… city", "located at the middle" — [same](https://www.mextesol.net/journal/public/files/2996274430055836fdb4163281d420ec.pdf)

**Vocabulary and lexical studies**
- **Can 2022 (WEAK, unpublished conference paper) [abstract]:** 100 ELT undergraduates. Nearly a quarter of lexical errors came from confused sense relations, about 20% from wrong prepositions, and under 15% from collocation problems. Some calques were attributed to L1 — [TEDU record](https://avesis.tedu.edu.tr/yayin/0c62db4a-2e4a-4406-b1ef-389f59aae023/common-lexical-errors-and-the-possible-effect-of-l1-turkish)
- **Cangır 2023 (WEAK, conference abstract) [abstract]:** 350 essays, six raters. Semantic, collocational and prepositional errors dominated. The main sources were near-synonym confusion, L1 transfer and typological difference. No frequencies reported — [Ankara record](https://avesis.ankara.edu.tr/yayin/3b613f13-dd2a-4b24-953b-935ea794ea8e/major-sources-of-lexical-errors-in-second-language-writing-in-the-turkish-context)

**Articles: direction and level**
- **Koban Koç 2015 [abstract]:** 50 intermediate and 50 low-advanced prep students, essays, Hacettepe University. All omitted "the" more than they overused it, and omission fell with level — [HÜ Eğitim Dergisi 30(2)](https://avesis.hacettepe.edu.tr/yayin/96d7e6c5-62a2-4a63-acce-2e45b08d1423/the-non-generic-use-of-the-definite-article-the-in-writing-by-turkish-learners-of-english)
- **Atay 2010, METU thesis [snippet]:** intermediate learners fluctuated strongly between definiteness and specificity, while elementary learners omitted articles in definite contexts — [METU record](https://avesis.metu.edu.tr/yonetilen-tez/e79b5a86-31db-4df0-b735-4a05dd5de83b/second-language-acquisition-of-the-english-article-system-by-turkish-learners-the-role-of-semantic-notions); contradicted by a [50-learner study that did not support the Fluctuation Hypothesis [snippet]](https://openaccess.mku.edu.tr/items/73da8e7e-e29e-4ddf-88c0-efb97b66a057)
- A Boğaziçi thesis found overuse of the and a/an in zero-article contexts [snippet] — [Boğaziçi archive](https://digitalarchive.library.bogazici.edu.tr/bitstreams/6f124e43-bfaa-4e98-9317-cad0b9d500be/download)

**Tense and aspect**
- **Zıngır, Anadolu University thesis [snippet]:** past progressive and present tenses were problematic for intermediate learners — [Anadolu archive](https://acikerisim.anadolu.edu.tr/bitstreams/114fbeeb-10f9-4f8f-8ad1-0cbfb2c203b3/download)
- **Brenner, Bilkent MA thesis [snippet]:** present perfect used for simple past made up 23.02% of tense-aspect errors, and omitted auxiliary "be" made up 63.41% of progressive misuse. The URL match is unverified because the record returned 403 — [Tezara record](https://tezara.org/theses/26837)

**Prepositions**
- **Özışık 2014 (WEAK, n=30, test not corpus) [abstract]:** upper-intermediate learners on a 60-item gap-fill had great difficulty, with many errors attributed to L1 — [İKÜ record](https://openaccess.iku.edu.tr/handle/11413/3613)
- **Özbay & Bozkurt 2017 [abstract]:** complex prepositions in KTUCLE compared with native LOCNESS show overuse and underuse patterns, with no error counts on the page — [IJER 8(3)](https://ijer.inased.org/makale/387)
- **Gökçeli 2019 on in/on/at in ICLE data [snippet]:** errors linked to -de interference and overgeneralisation. The source URL was not confirmed, and the study appeared only in a [search-result listing](https://www.anadiliegitimi.com/en/pub/eltrj/issue/59207/818769)
- **MEXTESOL 2023 (WEAK, n=19 high school) [snippet]:** omission was the leading category and prepositions the most error-prone element — [MEXTESOL](https://mextesol.net/journal/public/files/2aa772ff6244d053d5e096f1ff48aa14.pdf)

**TICLE, pro-drop and other tagged items**
- **Evirgen 2018 BA thesis using TICLE [abstract]:** targets subject-verb agreement and missing verbs, with no frequencies on the page — [GRIN](https://www.grin.com/document/451648)
- Pro-drop is studied by Kuru Gönen 2010, but the record gave no sample or results — [Anadolu record](https://avesis.anadolu.edu.tr/yayin/eeddc397-f760-4da7-8766-362acb84e346/pro-drop-parameter-and-l1-transfer-a-study-on-turkish-speakers-of-english). A Mehmet Akif record frames it as parameter resetting at initial, intermediate and advanced levels, also without numbers — [record](https://acikerisim.mehmetakif.edu.tr/items/89a325f3-19b8-4610-8065-b4139e751a9b)

### Inferences
- **Suggested frequency weights for the error model (my synthesis):**
  - High: verb form and agreement, prepositions, articles (both directions), noun number, word choice.
  - Medium: tense choice, fragments, word form.
  - Low in advanced writing, but likely higher in beginner speech: word order, as misordering was under 5% in LECOSE.
- **Tags to add or split:**
  - Article omission, redundant article and "the another" as separate tags. Direction shifts with level, so a single tag hides progress.
  - Third-person -s split into omission ("he go") and overuse ("those who downloads"), plus do/have agreement ("this student do not").
  - Double marking ("doesn't likes"), and verb form misselection ("buyed", "I seen", "are lefting").
  - Auxiliary hybrids ("I've been lived") and present perfect used for simple past.
  - Missing preposition after a movement verb ("go a cold city"), separately from in/on/at.
  - for/to purpose confusion (için).
  - there are + singular noun, and many/lots of + singular, as a distinct noun-number context.
  - Because-fragments and run-ons for B1-B2 writing.
  - Lexical: near-synonym choice, verb-noun collocation, calque, and word form (adjective for adverb, or invented "systematical").
  - Embedded question order ("tell me where did you go"), which was an example category in the LECOSE scheme, though no specific count was read.
  - Punctuation as an optional writing tag, based only on the small Erkaya study.
- **Feedback design:** the LECOSE result suggests marking errors alone may not reduce them over a term. Spaced productive practice on the tagged item looks more defensible than correction messages alone, though this is untested here.
- **Age and level caution:** nearly all data come from university writing. Spoken and beginner data are largely missing, so to-be omission, SOV order and he/she rates can't be set from these studies. Calibrate them from the app's own logs.

### Gaps
- No published TICLE error frequencies or corpus size were found. The ICLE documentation I reached contained no such data.
- I found no frequency study of copula omission, he/she confusion or subject pro-drop. These are widely assumed from Turkish structure, but I have no measured rate. Only a [learner-resource note on genderless "o"](https://elon.io/grammar/turkish/pronouns/personal) surfaced for he/she, and it does not test the claim.
- A search summary reported a study where articles were low-frequency (n=19), but I could not trace it, so I left it out as evidence.
- I found nothing Turkish-specific and measured on gerund vs infinitive, relative-clause avoidance, conditionals, passive or reported speech, beyond "voice" being fifth in LECOSE.
- Full-text numbers for Kırkgöz 2010 ("An analysis of written errors of Turkish adult learners"), [ED600674](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED600674) and Tunaz et al. 2016 were not reachable.

## What are the pronunciation problems?

### Takeaway
/θ/, /ð/, /w/-/v/, /ŋ/, schwa and several vowels recur, but most studies are small or use teacher trainees. The best-measured perception data show /v/-/w/ and /ɛ/-/æ/ are weaker than /i/-/ɪ/. Evidence on devoicing and clusters is thin but points the expected way.

### Cited Findings

**Consonants**
- **Topal & Altay 2022 [full text]:** 49 prospective teachers, POSE perception test, 7 raters on read-aloud speech. These are high-level learners, so basic learners are likely worse (my inference) — [IOJET 9(4)](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1353592.pdf)
- Consonant perception averaged 94%, against a 75% threshold. Weakest contrasts: /v/-/w/ initial 70%; /θ/-/t/ initial 76%; /s/-/z/ final 82%; /p/-/b/ final 83%; /ð/-/d/ initial 84% (only 2 items); /k/-/g/ final 85%; /f/-/θ/ initial 86% — [same](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1353592.pdf)
- Expert ratings (1-9 scale): /θ/ was highly problematic (M=7.55), and /ŋ/, /ð/ and /w/ were moderately problematic (M=5.27). The paper also says /θ/, /ð/ and /w/ do not exist in Turkish — [same](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1353592.pdf)
- **Ercan 2018 (WEAK, n=30 high school, Northern Cyprus) [abstract]:** serious difficulty with /θ/, /ð/, /w/, /v/, /ŋ/, /ɪ/, /ʊ/, /ɔː/, /əʊ/, /aʊ/. No per-sound rates on the abstract page. Causes given: L1 interference, limited exposure, weak pronunciation training, English spelling — [IOJET 5(4)](https://iojet.org/index.php/IOJET/article/view/457)
- Topal & Altay also compile earlier work listing dark /l/ (Kahraman 2013) and /dʒ/, /tʃ/, /ʃ/, /ʒ/, /r/ (Demirezen and others) as problematic. Their own raters rated /ʒ/, /dʒ/, /ʃ/, /tʃ/ only slightly problematic, so the literature conflicts — [same](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1353592.pdf)

**Final devoicing**
- In the Topal & Altay data, voicing was consistently weaker in final than initial position: /p/-/b/ 83% final vs 100% initial; /k/-/g/ 85% final vs 99% initial. But /t/-/d/ final was 95% — [same](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1353592.pdf)

**Vowels**
- Vowel perception in the same study averaged 84%. Weakest contrasts: /ɑ/-/ə/ 52%; /ɛ/-/æ/ 60%; /ɑ/-/oʊ/ 76%; /ə/-/ʊ/ 82%. /i/-/ɪ/ was **not** problematic in perception, which departs from earlier studies — [same](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1353592.pdf)
- **Demirezen 2017 [snippet]:** 39 pre-intermediate English majors had 66.67% perception of [ɪ]. This conflicts with the Topal & Altay finding on /i/-/ɪ/, probably due to level — [IJERS](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/ijers/article/520622)
- **Şen 2019 (WEAK, n=20) [snippet]:** a teaching approach for /iː/, /ɪ/, /ʊ/, /uː/, with a Turkish-English vowel system contrast — [ELOPE](https://doaj.org/article/8efd406770ae47a59409b6a30744d565)
- Words most often marked unintelligible in read-aloud by non-native rater-teachers: wolf (1801 marks), shepherd, hill, youth, chasing, comfort, again, grumbling, sheep, flock, naughty, truth, said, alarmed, away, thought. The authors link these to /ʊ/, /ə/, dark /l/, /θ/, /ŋ/, /iː/, /ɑː/, /ɒ/, /ɔ/, /ɛ/ — [Topal & Altay](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1353592.pdf)
- Despite this, the same speakers were rated moderately accented (M=4.77) and easy to understand (M=1.95, 1=easy). The authors say these familiar-accent raters may be lenient — [same](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1353592.pdf)

**Clusters and epenthesis**
- **Yavaş & Altan 2016 (WEAK, n=25) [snippet]:** in English #sC clusters, /st/ and /sn/ were produced best, with /sp/ and /sk/ ranked below them. Frequency did not explain the pattern — [John Benjamins](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/books/9789027266965-slcs.175.01yav)
- A 2021 analytic (non-experimental) paper states Turkish disallows word-initial clusters, with an epenthetic high vowel inserted in loanwords, and predicts transfer to English — [IJES via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/9c649dd6324c4d44a1cd6cf24c08b92c)
- An ICPhS 2023 paper reports that L2 fluency seemed to help Turkish speakers discriminate word-initial CC from VCV tokens, which suggests a perceptual illusion is a factor [snippet] — [ICPhS 2023](https://guarant.cz/icphs2023/745.pdf)

**Stress, rhythm and weak forms**
- **Levis & Uzun 2025 (PSLLT 16, n=40 Ankara) [snippet]:** read-aloud stress errors were partly from Turkish transfer. Learners skipped vowel reduction and used spelling-based pronunciation. Higher proficiency meant fewer stress errors — [PSLLT](https://www.iastatedigitalpress.com/psllt/article/id/21224/)
- **Uzun 2024 [snippet]:** word stress accuracy fell as syllable count rose — [AJAL via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/ff31e537fdd142a99c962f15e0989ba0)
- **Serin 2017 [snippet]:** polysyllable stress errors attributed to Turkish stress patterns — [Bayburt record](https://openaccess.bayburt.edu.tr/items/1b385b23-12b6-4a03-9f0c-491b502afcb4)
- **Topal 2024 [snippet]:** poor knowledge, perception and production of suprafixes — [Gazi record](https://avesis.gazi.edu.tr/yayin/101470b1-c426-4f67-96c1-6e971c68c7ac/turkish-efl-learners-knowledge-perception-and-production-of-suprafixes-as-part-of-lexical-competence)
- **Güzel (WEAK, practice piece) [snippet]:** Turkish listeners struggle with schwa in function words — [IBU eprints](https://eprints.ibu.edu.ba/items/show/2506)

### Inferences
- **Pronunciation priority for the app (my ranking):**
  1. Schwa and reduction in function words and unstressed syllables, as this also drives listening failure.
  2. /v/-/w/.
  3. /θ/ and /ð/, which are salient but may matter less for intelligibility. Topal & Altay's functional-load figures are modest (/θ/-/t/ initial 18%, /ð/-/d/ 19%), though their highest-load contrasts were not the problematic ones.
  4. /ɛ/-/æ/, /ʊ/ in "wolf"-type words, and /ŋ/.
  5. Spelling-to-sound habits, a broad cause that drill sets on single phonemes won't fix.
- **Spelling matters:** Turkish speakers appear to read English as spelled. A "said", "again", "comfort", "naughty" list is a cheap high-value exercise.
- **Devoicing:** teach it as a perception and production pair on tag-and-test items like cap/cab, ice/eyes, tack/tag.
- **Tense-lax:** do not assume /i/-/ɪ/ is the top problem at all levels. Run a short POSE-style placement test instead.
- **Epenthesis:** start with /sk/ and /sp/ onsets (school, speak, sport).

### Gaps
- No measured Turkish-specific rates of prothesis ([ɪskul]) vs anaptyxis were found, and I did not read Yavaş & Altan's full results.
- No study directly tested Turkish final-stress transfer or linked Turkish rhythm to English weak forms.
- No study measured final devoicing in production by Turkish EFL learners. The cited data are perception only.
- Full texts of Levis & Uzun and Uzun 2024 were blocked (bot protection / 403).

## What are the false friends and L1-transfer vocabulary and collocation errors?

### Takeaway
Measured work points to verb+noun and preposition collocations plus near-synonyms. False-friend lists exist mainly on low-authority sites, so they are good seed content but need editorial checking.

### Cited Findings
- **Bıçkı 2012, as cited in a verb-collocation paper [snippet]:** verb+NP was the most prevalent collocational error group — [MKÜ open access](https://openaccess.mku.edu.tr/bitstreams/303201bb-629c-45ff-bec8-aadaaafa8ff7/download)
- An experimental study found learners scored higher on congruent than non-congruent delexical verb collocations, and that assumed similarity caused errors [snippet] — [Int. Journal of English Studies](https://revistas.um.es/ijes/article/download/212631/195731)
- **A 1993 thesis (WEAK, n=36 beginner Azeri and Turkish learners) [abstract]:** make/do interference errors came mainly from the semantic structures of collocations. Old and mixed L1 — [Bilkent](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/items/fecc1e8c-0048-429e-9d3a-1acb881fab4f)
- A Turkish study identified 100 partial false friends in English-Turkish diplomatic texts [snippet]. The genre is specialised — [IEU](https://gcris.ieu.edu.tr/bitstream/20.500.14365/4494/1/3558.pdf)
- **Wikiversity list (WEAK, community-edited):** aktüel (topical) vs actual; apartman (building) vs apartment; formasyon (teacher training) vs formation; kanalizasyon (sewerage) vs canalization; petrol (crude oil) vs British petrol (gasoline); market (store) vs market; tente (awning) vs tent; tost vs toast; keten (linen) vs cotton — [Wikiversity](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/TESOL/False_Friends_in_Turkish)
- **Lemon Academy guide (WEAK, commercial):** actual/aktüel; sympathetic/sempatik; fabric/fabrika; sensible/sensitif; preservative/prezervatif. Some entries on this page are garbled (library, lecture, eventually), so do not copy without editing — [Lemon Academy](https://lemonacademy.co.uk/?p=61725)
- pasta means cake in Turkish, per a learner resource — [Elon](https://elon.io/learn-hardcore-turkish/lexicon/373172/ben-pasta-yapıyorum.)
- sigara (cigarette) vs cigar is listed by Talkpal. The same page wrongly lists pantolon, which shows why these lists need vetting — [Talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/?p=1577591)
- Calques and near-synonym confusion are the leading lexical-error sources in the [Can 2022](https://avesis.tedu.edu.tr/yayin/0c62db4a-2e4a-4406-b1ef-389f59aae023/common-lexical-errors-and-the-possible-effect-of-l1-turkish) and [Cangır 2023](https://avesis.ankara.edu.tr/yayin/3b613f13-dd2a-4b24-953b-935ea794ea8e/major-sources-of-lexical-errors-in-second-language-writing-in-the-turkish-context) abstracts
- Lexical examples from Erkaya: "smooth area", "successful in some biological subjects", "available city" — [MEXTESOL](https://www.mextesol.net/journal/public/files/2996274430055836fdb4163281d420ec.pdf)

### Inferences
- Add an error tag family: `false_friend`, `calque`, `verb_noun_collocation`, `dependent_preposition`, `near_synonym`. Make/do is one case of a bigger delexical verb problem and could extend to take/have/give.
- **Seed list for editors to verify:** actual, apartment, sympathetic, sensible, fabric, formation, market, petrol, pasta, cigar, preservative.
- Treat a false-friend hit as a lexical error, not a grammar one, so analytics don't blur them.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed frequency study of false friends in general-English Turkish learner data.
- I could not verify a candidate "entelektüel" pair; no source surfaced.
- No Turkish-English collocation frequency list was found. The app may need to mine its own logs.

## What about anxiety, why does school English leave speaking weak, what does EF EPI say, and what do the exams demand?

### Takeaway
Speaking is Turkey's weakest measured skill (EF EPI 2025: speaking 438, reading 503), and national exams reward reading and vocabulary, not speech. Anxiety is real but its reported level varies by study, so the design goal is low-stakes speaking, not an assumption that every learner is highly anxious.

### Cited Findings

**EF EPI (latest edition found, 2025)**
- Türkiye ranked #71 with a score of 488, equal to the global average of 488 and down 9 points. Skills: reading 503, listening 478, writing 441, speaking 438 — [EF fact sheet 2025](https://www.ef.com/assetscdn/WIBIwq6RdJvcD9bc8RMd/cefcom-epi-site/fact-sheets/2025/ef-epi-fact-sheet-turkey-english.pdf)
- 71st of 123, from 65th of 116 and a score of 497 in 2024; "low" band; second-to-last in Europe. The sample was 2.2 million test takers, with an average age of 26 — [Karar](https://www.karar.com/guncel-haberler/ingilizcede-sinifta-kaldik-ef-2025-endeksine-gore-turkiye-avrupada-2007928)
- Cities: İzmir 515, Antalya 510, Ankara 508, İstanbul 504, Eskişehir 503, Samsun 485, Bursa 483, Konya 467, Adana-Mersin 466, Gaziantep 461, Kayseri 461. Regions: Marmara 500, Aegean 498, Central Anatolia 496, Mediterranean 484, Black Sea 481, Southeast 448, East 446 — [EF](https://www.ef.com/epi/regions/europe/turkey/)
- Job function: students 489, teachers 537, customer service 538, R&D 519, IT 513, accounting & finance 445, HR 441, technicians 434 — [EF fact sheet 2025](https://www.ef.com/assetscdn/WIBIwq6RdJvcD9bc8RMd/cefcom-epi-site/fact-sheets/2025/ef-epi-fact-sheet-turkey-english.pdf)

**School English**
- **TEPAV/British Council 2013:** fieldwork February-July 2013, about 21,000 surveyed; state-school students stayed at basic level after about 1,000 hours of instruction [snippet] — [Sabancı forum note](https://sl.sabanciuniv.edu/en/node/316). TEPAV elsewhere says "almost 20,000" — [TEPAV](https://tepav.org.tr/en/haberler/s/3635)
- Sample details in the report foreword: 78 classes observed in 48 schools across 12 cities, 87 teacher interviews [snippet]; a separate summary says 80 observations — [British Council PDF](https://www.britishcouncil.org.tr/sites/default/files/turkey_national_needs_assessment_of_state_school_english_language_teaching.pdf)
- **Commentary (opinion, Güven Sak, 2014):** the same grammar cycle repeats across school stages, with teacher-led rule explanation and error correction. Exams test grammar and there is little discussion or essay work. 21% of primary and 32% of secondary pupils self-rate as beginners, and 10% of primary pupils self-rate as advanced, about double the secondary figure — [TEPAV blog](https://tepav.org.tr/blog/s/4604)
- The British Council's 2015 higher-education baseline covered 38 universities in 15 cities and recommended moving to general academic English [snippet] — [British Council PDF](https://www.britishcouncil.org.tr/sites/default/files/he_baseline_study_book_web_-_son.pdf)
- **Polat:** YDS and YÖKDİL don't test listening, speaking or writing, which shapes learner habits. Most participants passed but were dissatisfied with non-reading skills [snippet] — [Polat PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Murat-Polat-2/publication/346000519_YDS_ve_YOKDIL_Sinavlarinin_Yabanci_Dil_Egitimi_Uzerine_Etkileri/links/60995f9492851c490fce8764/YDS-ve-YOeKDIL-Sinavlarinin-Yabanci-Dil-Egitimi-Uezerine-Etkileri.pdf)

**Exams**
- **YÖKDİL (ÖSYM guide 2026, primary) [full text]:** 80 multiple-choice questions in 180 minutes, covering vocabulary, grammar, translation and reading comprehension. Separate English science, health and social sciences booklets. Only correct answers count, wrong answers are ignored, score out of 100. Fee 1,200 TL. Dates: 8 March and 9 August 2026 — [ÖSYM guide](https://dokuman.osym.gov.tr/pdfdokuman/2026/YOKDIL-2/kilavuz_ykdl2d16062026.pdf)

Sites disagree on section counts (6 vs 15 vocabulary questions), so check the ÖSYM guide — [Atlas Rehberlik](https://www.atlasrehberlik.com/2026-yds-yabanci-dil-sinavi-konulari-ve-soru-dagilimi/); [Sınavtime](https://sinavtime.com/2026-yds-konulari-ve-soru-dagilimi/)

### Inferences
- **Two user jobs need separate tracks:** (a) exam-driven YDS/YÖKDİL users, who need academic vocabulary, grammar-in-context, translation and paragraph strategy (no audio, no speaking); and (b) speaking-driven users, who need what school did not provide. IELTS and TOEFL users overlap with both.
- **Speaking gap as the positioning angle:** reading exceeds speaking by 65 EF points. EF's sample is self-selected online test takers, and speaking scores may be estimated by its own method, so use it as a direction, not a precise measure.
- **Reassurance over assumption:** anxiety results range from low to moderate depending on instrument. Private AI speaking with no audience fits the Çağatay finding that anxiety rises with native speakers, but I found no Turkish study testing an AI partner.
- **Regional targeting:** the east-southeast gap (about 50 points below Marmara) suggests beginner-friendly content must not assume big-city exposure.
- **YÖKDİL's no-penalty scoring** means guessing strategy is valid exam content.

### Inferences
- **Affordability math (my calculation; prices are 2025, wage 2026):** Cambly at 2 lessons a week is about 6.7% of a net minimum wage. Wall Street at 4,000-6,000 a month is about 14-21%. An exam sitting is about 4.3%. Even so, a 2025 Turkish Duolingo price under €2 a month is the mental anchor for "an app."
- **Pricing hypothesis:** an app priced per month well below one tutoring lesson (300-800 TL per lesson) can be framed against tutoring, not against Duolingo, if it offers speaking and correction. Test annual vs monthly and student pricing. Treat these as hypotheses.
- **Payments:** regional store pricing and lira volatility mean any fixed euro or dollar logic will drift. Review local price tiers regularly.
- **Technical:** bandwidth is rarely the constraint for most users. Speech features seem feasible, though older phones and rural quality need checking. The median-speed figures hide that spread.
